Good Work Takes Care

Nov 2026

It’s been a few months since I joined [Redacted until Public]. Since then, I’ve been endeavoring as Chief of AI (you could imagine my surprise) and a principal researcher here, focusing on research around interpretability, data, and some really intriguing pretraining ideas. Hopefully, I can share them soon.

Though I had a leadership role previously at Ani.ML health as a senior engineer, things are working a lot differently here — faster, bigger scale, more things that need to be done just right. With it comes the weight of my colleagues as their fate lies in the hands of the daunting nature of my leadership. Although I try my best to imbue intellectual rigor, it’s sometimes easy for compassion and kindness to disappear in the face of rapid, nigh unimaginable progress.

Whenever I reflect, I find myself incredibly grateful and lucky to have received such generous, caring and compassionate mentorship from Dr. Kuan Cheun Wu (from Ani.ML Health and Inspirit) and Alex Warstadt (UCSD) as of late. A lot of my fondest memories include working together and spending hours together patching kernel issues in our EC2 instances, figuring out syntax trees, discussing better approaches to research and learning as a whole. To be fortunate enough as to have met almost every author I’d cited in my paper for ACL at ACL 2026, and the rising wonder and excitement in my chest speaking with every one of you. I’m in a very different environment right now, sprinting at the pace of industry while trying to maintain the rigorously kind, understanding, and intellectual environment to nurture good research.

As I recall reading from Eric Zhang’s blog in January, as things were beginning to accelerate:

There’s a pressure to be productive earlier, and even those few weeks of on-ramp on any given thing hurt execution speed. I hear more conversations about “have they done it before” rather than “how can we do something amazing?” — the predictability is sought after.

Despite this pressure, I hope to write in candor as a simple reminder to myself.

It’s moving to see so many people here, so passionate and bright and excited. …And I feel an ever growing cherishment of the intellectual lives of my coworkers whom every one I’m glad to call my friends (many of which are my closest friends) — the things they find beautiful, questions that occupy their minds, and aspirations that shape their lives. Whether it's painting, music, skydiving, silly app side projects, running, or writing poetry and letters, it reminds me of a feeling I can only describe as childlike wonder that grounds our work as uniquely human in the face of superhuman autonomy. I adore their earnest curiosity and caring nature towards each other and the craft of it all — let’s keep things this way.

It’s exciting to watch from and race towards the frontier, and it’s easy to get lost in it.

Just as good research takes time, good work takes care.
